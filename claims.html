Field guide · Weeks 7 & 10

How claims harden

The evidence-status card sorts kinds of confidence. This page tracks a different axis: how a statement moves toward — or away from — being treated as background. Status answers what you possess. Hardening answers what the community has stopped arguing about.

A working ladder

Not a ranking of truth. A vocabulary for how much contingency is still visible in the statement.

  1. 1
    Open conjectureSpeculation offered as such. Entitled to be wrong without repairing a shared background.
  2. 2
    Hedged report“Thought to be,” “appears,” “in these runs.” The hedge is load-bearing; stripping it changes the claim.
  3. 3
    Modalized claimAn assertion that still shows its conditions: named methods, citations, assessors, limits of scope.
  4. 4
    Modality-free assertionStated as fact in the discourse at hand. Contingency has been deleted from the sentence, not necessarily from the world.
  5. 5
    Background assumptionRarely stated at all. Used rather than defended. Often invisible until someone points at the production history.

Caution — erasure looks like certainty. A statement hardens when persuasion succeeds so thoroughly that the writing, checking, and apparatus drop out of view. Pointing back at that history is one way to soften a claim without inventing a rival theory.

Operations students can name

  • Add a modality — restore conditions, named checkers, or scope limits to a type-4 sentence.
  • Delete a modality — the ordinary path of textbook writing; ask what was removed and who was entitled to remove it.
  • Challenge independence — two “assessed records” that share an unnoticed source are one record with an echo (see the evidence-status caution).
  • Reopen the inscription chain — ask which durable traces (graphs, logs, typed edges, sealed files) stand in for the work, and what upstream cost they hide.

Relation to evidence statuses

A result can be formally checked and still sit at rung 2 in a human-facing summary (“the checker accepted it under assumptions we cannot audit”). A result can be presently inaccessible as explanation and yet function as rung 5 inside a machine community that treats it as infrastructure. The two vocabularies cross; neither replaces the other.

Internal audiences

Week 8 asks what communities create for one another. Hardening often happens there first: a distinction becomes background for participants long before any human liaison can classify its evidence status. Treat that lag as data, not as failure of the human curriculum. For the channel map (primary / pidgin / liaison) see Internal dialects and the liaison surface.

Seminar prompt

Take one sentence from the two-accounts exercise (or from a sealed note in your own fieldwork). Place it on the ladder as it appears in the human-facing summary, then again as it appears in the internal sample. Name one operation that would move it one rung in either direction — and what evidence would justify that move.

Historical method note (optional): twentieth-century laboratory ethnography described similar ladders for scientific papers and bench talk. The course uses the pattern because it travels; it does not require those sources to grade.
